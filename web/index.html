NASA Space Apps 2026 · Flame in Freefall

The Moon base will breathe 30% oxygen.

Here is what NASA's own flight data says about fire in that air, and where that data runs out. Skylab Phoenix cleans every combustion experiment in NASA's Physical Sciences Informatics repository, ranks the 24 investigations for each crewed habitat, runs validated models with explicit uncertainty, and writes briefings in which every sentence cites the data and every number is checked by code.

Five crewed atmospheres

Same flame, five atmospheres. The chance a fuel-droplet fire puts itself out, from NASA's FLEX flight data.

Methanol droplet, no suppressant. Droplet flames are what FLEX flew; solid cabin materials have no flight data in these atmospheres. The model says “extrapolating” when any input leaves the flown range and withholds a number far from the data. Select an atmosphere to open it in the simulator.

Why it matters

Apollo 1, Apollo 13, Mir: each one involved oxygen. The Moon base will breathe more of it.

    02 · Coverage

    Where NASA has flown fire. And where crews will live.

    Every flight test with a known oxygen level and pressure, against the atmospheres of crewed habitats. The lunar-base envelope from the challenge brief (≥30% O₂, ≤10.2 psi) is almost empty, and no solid material has ever burned there in microgravity.

    05 · Solid materials

    Cabin materials in orbit. They keep burning at less oxygen than Earth tests suggest.

    Fabrics and plastics are what actually burns in a spacecraft. BASS and BASS-II burned PMMA rods, slabs and films, SIBAL fabric and Nomex in the ISS glovebox while astronauts varied the airflow; Saffire-I to -III burned large samples on Cygnus. Here are the measured limits, the gases those fires released, and what they imply for each habitat.

    Challenge background · flammability studies focus on solid materials such as fabrics and plastics (BASS, BASS-II, Saffire)

    09 · On Earth

    Space fire research comes home. Detectors, air tanks, suits and water mist.

    The challenge asks how PSI data can also make fire safer on Earth. NASA technology already did: smoke detection, firefighter air tanks and protective clothing, and fine-water-mist extinguishers developed for the ISS. The same data in this dashboard points to more.

    Challenge objective · potential applications of the PSI data to fire safety on Earth

    10 · Data & validation

    Reproducible pipeline. Honest validation.

    One command rebuilds everything from NASA's public API. Every correction to NASA's tables is logged, and the model is scored only on test days it never saw.

      Model vs baselines

      Held-out validation.

      AUC: ranking quality (0.5 = chance). Brier and log loss: calibration of the probabilities (lower is better). The simpler logistic model beats gradient boosting on held-out days, so it is the one deployed.

      Predicted vs observed.

      SI verification firewall

      Every briefing, checked.

      Checks per sentence: at least one citation to an existing evidence item; every number present in the cited items; no safety verdict (“is safe”, “certified”, “guaranteed”…); the evidence pack unchanged since the draft was written. A briefing that fails shows the evidence itself, labelled “Evidence brief”. Regenerate with python scripts/si_generate.py.
