The Moon base will breathe 30% oxygen.

NASA has flown about 1,600 fires in orbit. Only a few dozen came near that air, and no solid material has ever burned there. Skylab Phoenix turns NASA's combustion archive into fire-safety answers you can check, and says plainly where the data runs out.

      01 · The problem

      Five crewed atmospheres. The same fire behaves differently in each.

      The chance that a fuel-droplet fire puts itself out, from NASA's FLEX flight data. More oxygen at lower pressure saves mass on the Moon, and it also takes away a fire's tendency to die on its own.

      Methanol droplet, no suppressant. The model says “extrapolating” when an input leaves the flown range and withholds a number far from the data. Select an atmosphere to open it in the simulator.

      Why it matters

      Apollo 1, Apollo 13, Mir: each one involved oxygen. The Moon base will breathe more of it.

        03 · The gap

        Where NASA has flown fire. And where crews will live.

        Every flight test with a known oxygen level and pressure, against the crewed atmospheres. The lunar-base envelope from the brief (≥30% O₂, ≤10.2 psi) is almost empty.

        Hollow markers: O₂ or pressure not reported per test, taken as ISS cabin air. Crossed markers: inverse flames, where the O₂ value is the oxidizer stream from the burner. ACME BRE (26.5 / 34 / 40% O₂) is not plotted because its table does not report pressure.

        04 · Solid materials

        Cabin materials in orbit. They keep burning at less oxygen than Earth tests suggest.

        Fabrics and plastics are what actually burns in a spacecraft. BASS-II burned PMMA rods, slabs, films and fabrics in the ISS glovebox while astronauts varied the airflow. These are NASA's measured limits and the gases those fires released.

        NASA flammability boundary · PMMA rods · 1 atm

          SI verification firewall

          Every briefing, checked.

          Checks per sentence: at least one citation to an existing evidence item; every number present in the cited items; no safety verdict (“is safe”, “certified”, “guaranteed”…); the evidence pack unchanged since the draft was written. A briefing that fails shows the evidence itself, labelled “Evidence brief”. Regenerate with python scripts/si_generate.py.
